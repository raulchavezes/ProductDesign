01 — Situation

$12.5M lost every year — to documents no one could find.

Google's DCOps teams had a systemic documentation problem. Based on industry benchmark data, DCOps was losing $12.5 million per year in labor hours — not due to lack of content, but due to an inability to find and effectively use the documentation that already existed.

Building documentation utilized by DCOps on a daily basis lacked structured metadata, making it effectively unsearchable. Teams were duplicating effort, making decisions without the right context, and spending hours hunting for information that should have been instantly accessible.

The core problem

Documentation existed but couldn't be found. No unified search, no metadata structure, no single source of truth.

The constraint

Solo designer on the project. Required gathering all requirements from stakeholders and SMEs, then designing the full MVP independently.

"DCOps wastes $12.5M in labor hours per year due to poorly managed content."

The goal wasn't to create more content — it was to make existing content finally usable.

02 — My Role

Solo designer. From zero to a shipped product.

I was the only Interaction Designer assigned to OpsMind — responsible for the entire design arc: from gathering requirements through stakeholder workshops and SME discussions, to crafting all MVP flows, to delivering production-ready screens for the engineering team.

This was a 0→1 platform build with a hard launch deadline of early 2025. There was no design precedent to follow — I worked hand in hand with the development team to craft a custom frontend that could connect to multiple backend systems while presenting a single, unified search experience to users.

Stakeholder Workshops Facilitated discovery sessions with stakeholders and SMEs to extract requirements and validate assumptions.
Unified Search UX Designed the AI-powered search experience connecting multiple backend systems into a single interface.
MVP & Production Screens Full p0 and p1 flows — from low-fi to production-ready screens annotated for engineering handoff.
Metadata Architecture Designed the metadata tagging system enabling AI to extract, associate, and surface content accurately.

03 — Design Process

Requirements to launch — solo, structured, and shipped.

With no existing design system or product precedent, I had to build the process alongside the product — balancing rapid discovery with enough rigour to deliver a production-ready MVP by a fixed deadline.

01
Stakeholder Workshops & SME Discovery Facilitated structured workshops with DCOps stakeholders and subject matter experts to map current workflows, pain points, and document usage patterns. Built a shared understanding of the problem before touching any design tools.
02
Requirements Definition & Prioritization Synthesized workshop outputs into structured requirements with the product team. Separated p0 (launch-critical) from p1 (post-launch) features to protect scope and hit the early 2025 deadline.
03
Information Architecture & Search Design Designed the unified search experience — the core product value. Mapped how AI would extract and associate metadata from building documentation, and how users would navigate, filter, and verify results.
04
MVP Flow Design & Iteration Designed all critical flows from lo-fi to hi-fi — covering unified search, document preview, metadata management, and content remediation. Iterated closely with the engineering team to account for backend constraints.
05
Production Handoff & Launch Delivered production-ready screens with full annotations. Worked alongside engineering during build to catch implementation gaps. Platform launched in early 2025 on schedule.

04 — Results

Launched on time. $12.5M in annual savings unlocked.

OpsMind launched in early 2025, bringing together the best features of existing content management and document management platforms into one unified, AI-powered experience. For the first time, DCOps teams had a single search interface that actually surfaced what they needed.

The platform directly addressed the $12.5M annual productivity loss from poor documentation management — by enabling AI to extract, associate, and make metadata-rich documentation instantly searchable. A solo design effort, delivered on deadline, in production.
