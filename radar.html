01 — Situation

Flying blind across 100M+ data points.

Google's data center operations were running without adequate real-time visibility into their own infrastructure. Hardware and software failures — server crashes, power outages, network interruptions — could go completely unnoticed until they caused serious service disruptions or data loss.

Without a centralized monitoring platform, ops teams had no way to track critical metrics like CPU usage, memory load, disk space, or network throughput. There was no mechanism to detect performance degradation or bottlenecks before they reached users. The absence of a unified alerting system meant reactive firefighting instead of proactive prevention.

"You can't track what you can't see — and without visibility, degradation becomes disruption."

02 — My Role

Lead Interaction Designer, from requirements to production.

I worked directly with the lead developer and product owner to design and implement a new internal frontend for the anomaly detection and alerting platform. Beyond hands-on design, I managed work assignments for up to 6 engineering and UX contractors at a time — coordinating technical requirements across business users and engineering, and keeping the team aligned on priorities through the MVP and into production.

03 — Design Process

From ambiguity to production in structured phases.

The challenge was translating complex infrastructure monitoring data into actionable, scannable UX — for ops engineers who needed speed, clarity, and precision under pressure.

01
Requirements & Technical Scoping Coordinated with the lead developer and product owner to map technical constraints, data structures, and alerting rules. The fine-grained auth model required early alignment to avoid downstream redesigns.
02
Architecture & Information Design Defined the information architecture for the monitoring dashboard — organizing 100M+ data points into scannable hierarchies. Prioritized anomaly alerts, asset health, and real-time metric visibility as the core p0 flows.
03
MVP Flow Design Designed the MVP and critical interaction flows with the product team — covering anomaly detection views, alerting states, asset drill-down, and auth-based access levels. Iterated rapidly with lo-fi to hi-fi.
04
Production-Ready Screens Created production-ready, developer-annotated screens across p0 and p1 flows. Validated requirements with the lead developer and product owner before handoff to engineering.
05
Launch & Multi-Environment Deployment Supported launch across Production, Staging, and Autopush environments — verifying design integrity and flagging regressions through each deployment milestone.

04 — Results

Shipped to production. Across three environments.

Radar launched successfully in Production, Staging, and Autopush environments. The platform gave Google's data center ops teams their first real-time, unified view of infrastructure health — enabling proactive anomaly detection before failures reached users.

All p0 and p1 flows were validated, production-ready, and handed off to engineering with zero redesign cycles post-launch. The fine-grained auth system became a reference implementation for the team.
